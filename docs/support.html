Help and support

Getting the most from TypeParkinson.

Turning on the keyboard

  1. Open the Settings app › Apps › TypeParkinson › Keyboards, and turn on TypeParkinson. (Or Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards › Add New Keyboard.)
  2. Leave Allow Full Access off — TypeParkinson doesn’t need it.
  3. In any text field, tap or hold the globe key until TypeParkinson appears.

Common questions

The keyboard doesn’t appear in some apps.

iOS always uses its own keyboard for passwords, phone-number fields in some apps, and apps that block third-party keyboards. That’s expected; TypeParkinson comes back in other fields.

My changes in the app don’t show in the keyboard.

The keyboard reloads its settings each time it appears. Switch to another app and back, or tap the globe key twice.

Typing feels different today.

On the app’s home screen, tap Typing feels different today. Six taps compare today with your usual, and the keyboard reads your touches differently for the rest of the day. The keys don’t move. Tap Use usual to undo it.

Caps lock.

Tap shift once for one capital letter, a second time for caps lock, and a third time to turn it off. No quick double tap needed.

Holding delete erased too much.

Tap Undo — it brings back everything from one hold. You can turn hold-to-delete off in the app’s Settings.

Using it on another iPhone or iPad.

Sign in with Apple in the app’s Settings on both devices, with iCloud turned on. Your touch profile and keyboard follow you.

Speak and saved phrases

Open the Speak tab, type with your personal keyboard and tap Speak. Phrases lets you edit text you want to reuse. Use the card button for a large, full-screen message. Clear beside Said recently removes the recent spoken phrases kept on your device.

Sharing your history

Open Progress and choose PDF report for a one-page summary or Data (CSV) for the full history. Reports are made on your device; you choose where to send them. Activity readings describe what happened in a session and do not diagnose a condition.

Motion and voice practice

The tremor meter uses the motion sensors on a physical iPhone or iPad. Voice practice asks for microphone permission. Simulator recordings and screenshots on this website use sample data. Voice volume is relative to your phone’s microphone, not calibrated decibels.
