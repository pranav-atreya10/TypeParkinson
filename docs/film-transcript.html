A keyboard that learns your hands.

A text version of our silent, 94-second presentation. Watch the film.

  1. 0:00–0:07

    Every tap has an intention.

    “See you soon” types itself above a close-up keyboard. Two letters slip onto neighboring keys as the camera pulls back. You know what to say; your hands can make it harder.

  2. 0:07–0:14

    The problem

    An estimated 1.1 million Americans live with Parkinson’s. Tremor and stiffness can make a simple message harder to send. Source: Parkinson’s Foundation.

  3. 0:14–0:22

    TypeParkinson

    A large iPhone rises through a blue, purple and teal Aura. TypeParkinson: a keyboard that learns your hands. Built for iPhone and iPad.

  4. 0:22–0:34

    Your personal touch model

    Your hands have a pattern. Your keyboard can learn it. Illustrated touch dots cluster below and to the right of the intended key. Contours show their offset and spread; the intended key lights up as the model learns. The dots and keys stay in place.

  5. 0:34–0:48

    Smart correction

    An illustrated keyboard turns “tje” into “the,” “hellloo” into “hello,” “tomorow” into “tomorrow,” and “teh” into “the.” Individual letters move into the intended words above an angled keyboard. Corrections can be reverted. In the app, corrections use the actual touch pattern and run on the iPhone.

  6. 0:48–1:00

    Your everyday toolkit

    The camera travels across three large iPhones showing actual Today, Practice and Voice screens. The voice meter rises into its loud zone. A six-tap daily check, spiral tracing, rhythm tapping, tremor measurement, medication records, voice practice and Speak help build an everyday record. Screens and histories contain sample data.

  7. 1:00–1:10

    Movement, made visible

    A waveform becomes a frequency chart. Motion sensors measure shake strength. Fourier analysis finds shake rate. The chart’s peak near 5 Hz is illustrative, and the accompanying simulator reading is sample data. A measurement, not a diagnosis.

  8. 1:10–1:20

    Patterns to share

    The Progress screen and a report illustration separate into view. Your history and PDF and CSV exports support conversations with a care team. Sample histories are not clinical results or proof of a medication effect.

  9. 1:20–1:26

    Private by design

    On your iPhone. No servers. Your words stay yours. The keyboard never saves what you type. Optional sync uses your own iCloud.

  10. 1:26–1:34

    Built with purpose

    TypeParkinson. A keyboard that learns your hands. Atharv Sharma and Pranav Atreya. Congressional App Challenge.

TypeParkinson describes touches, movement and voice. It does not diagnose a condition or replace clinical care.
